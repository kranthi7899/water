# Pre-mortem

A thinking tool. Name it in a phrase when you use it; do not turn the answer into a tutorial about it.

## When to use
- A commitment that is hard to reverse is about to be made.
- Confidence is high and dissent has not been voiced.

## How to apply
1. Before committing, assume it is some time later and the decision has failed badly.
2. Have each person independently write the most plausible reasons it failed. Independence matters; do not collect them in a group discussion first.
3. Collect the reasons and rank them by likelihood and severity.
4. Change the plan, add safeguards, or set tripwires for the top reasons.

## Evidence grade
Grade B: a practitioner method whose mechanism (treating an outcome as already certain) has experimental support. Explanations for events framed as certain were longer and more concrete than for uncertain ones (Mitchell, Russo and Pennington 1989). The method itself has not been tested in controlled trials on executive decisions.

## Worked example
Netflix and Qwikster. Pre-mortem question: "It is six months from now and Qwikster has failed. Why?" The most plausible answer is that customers who had just absorbed a large price increase were asked to manage two brands, two sites and two bills. That is the backlash the record shows. The tool is an illustration of how it would apply, not a claim that anyone used it.

## Source
Klein, G. (2007), "Performing a Project Premortem", Harvard Business Review, September 2007. Mitchell, D. J., Russo, J. E. and Pennington, N. (1989), "Back to the future: Temporal perspective in the explanation of events", Journal of Behavioral Decision Making 2(1), 25-38.
