# WIP limits and flow efficiency

A thinking tool. Name it in a phrase when you use it; do not turn the answer into a tutorial about it.

## When to use
- Many items started, few finished.
- People juggling several items at once.
- Work stalling in handoffs more than in active work.

## How to apply
1. Set an explicit cap on items in progress per stage or per team, below the current level.
2. When the cap is reached, the rule is to finish or unblock existing work before starting new work.
3. Make the queue and the cap visible to everyone who pulls work, and feed delivery data back to the team.
4. Track flow efficiency (active working time divided by total elapsed time) to see how much time is spent waiting.

## Boundary condition
The DORA research reports that WIP limits drive improvement particularly when combined with visual displays and feedback from monitoring. Do not claim WIP limits alone improve delivery.

## Evidence grade
Grade B for WIP limits (survey-based research with the boundary condition above). Grade C for flow efficiency as a metric (a practitioner measure, not independently validated).

## Worked example
Constructed. Twelve features in progress across a five-person team means everyone touches several items. Capping at five and posting the board visibly means features finish one at a time instead of all advancing a little. The first completions arrive sooner even though nobody works faster.

## Source
Forsgren, N., Humble, J. and Kim, G. (2018), Accelerate: The Science of Lean Software and DevOps, IT Revolution. DORA, "Capabilities: Work in process limits", dora.dev.
