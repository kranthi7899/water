# Disagree and commit

A thinking tool. Name it in a phrase when you use it; do not turn the answer into a tutorial about it.

## When to use
- A leadership team is genuinely split.
- A deadline or the cost of delay is real.
- More discussion is unlikely to produce agreement.

## How to apply
1. Make sure the disagreement has actually been heard and the strongest version of each side has been stated.
2. If consensus comes, take it. If not, the decision owner decides.
3. Say explicitly that you are deciding despite unresolved disagreement, and why now.
4. Ask everyone to commit to executing the decision fully, and name what evidence would reopen it.

## Evidence grade
Grade C: practitioner-sourced (Bezos; commonly traced to Grove at Intel), not independently validated. Adjacent support is an inductive field study of eight firms in which most fast-deciding teams used "consensus with qualification": seek consensus, and if it does not come, the key decision maker decides with input from the team.

## Worked example
Intel exiting memory chips. The record shows internal identity still treated Intel as a memory company. An exit decision in that setting cannot wait for everyone to agree. It needs an explicit decision despite disagreement, and a commitment from the organization to execute it. An illustration, not a claim about what was said in the room.

## Source
Bezos, J. (2017), 2016 Letter to Shareholders, Amazon.com ("High-Velocity Decision Making"). Eisenhardt, K. M. (1989), "Making Fast Strategic Decisions in High-Velocity Environments", Academy of Management Journal 32(3), 543-576.
