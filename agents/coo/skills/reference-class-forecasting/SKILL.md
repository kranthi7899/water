# Reference-class forecasting

A thinking tool. Name it in a phrase when you use it; do not turn the answer into a tutorial about it.

## When to use
- A schedule or cost estimate built bottom-up by the people who will deliver it, especially for work that feels novel.
- A plan where the team's last similar efforts overran.

## How to apply
1. Name a reference class of genuinely comparable past efforts.
2. Establish the distribution of how they actually turned out against their original estimates.
3. Place this effort in that distribution, then apply an uplift to the inside-view estimate at the risk level you are willing to accept.
4. State the uplift explicitly next to the estimate, so reviewers see the adjustment, not just the number.

## Evidence grade
Grade B: peer-reviewed large-N observational research (258 projects in the underlying cost study).

## Worked example
Documented. The HHS Office of Inspector General found the agency prepared to launch HealthCare.gov "optimistic in spite of problems and never seriously considering delay" (OIG, OEI-06-14-00350, 2016). An estimate uplifted from how comparable large federal IT builds actually ran would have made the fixed date an explicit, visible risk rather than an assumption.
